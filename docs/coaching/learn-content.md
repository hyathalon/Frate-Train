# Learn — In-App Education Cards

**Purpose:** The app's "Learn" section, adapted from Coach Frates's athlete booklet. Every tier gets it. Each session's "Why this session" line links to the matching card. The weekly check-in chat answers athletes' questions from these cards and the coaching docs first, so fewer questions need a coach.

**Writing rules:** "Hyathlon", never "HYROX". Intensity as **RPE only** (the app's scale, below), never paces, splits or watts. No references to outside apps for moving sessions (that happens in the app). Keep each card short: a title, 3–6 short paragraphs or bullets, and a "Remember" line.

---

## A. How your program works

### A1. The principles behind your plan
- **Consistency (reversibility):** keep showing up. Fitness fades if training stops, and comes from stacking normal weeks.
- **Progressive overload:** we increase one thing at a time (a bit longer, a bit harder or a bit more often) so your body adapts without breaking down.
- **Specificity:** sessions look more and more like your event as it gets closer: running under fatigue, stations, race effort.
- **Individualisation:** your plan adjusts to your check-ins and logs. Tell the app how you're going: that's how it fits you.
- **Variety:** different sessions keep training interesting, spread the load across muscles and joints, and build a well-rounded athlete.
- **Active involvement:** the more you put in (logging, check-ins, effort on the right days), the more you get out.
**Remember:** go back to your why. The process takes care of the rest.

### A2. Hard days hard, easy days easy
The week has a rhythm of hard and easy days on purpose. Hard days create the stimulus. Easy days are where you absorb it, adapt and come back stronger.
The most common mistake is turning easy days into "moderate" days. That middle ground builds fatigue without building fitness.
**Remember:** push when it's time to push, and genuinely recover when it's time to recover.

### A3. Stacking your hard days
We put your hardest efforts together (e.g. a key run and a strength or Hyathlon session on the same day) so your other days can be true recovery days.
- Two sessions in a day: at least **6 hours apart** where possible.
- Your key run comes first. Heavy lower-body work before a quality run ruins the run.
- Never two interval sessions on back-to-back days.
**Remember:** concentrate the stress, then protect the recovery.

### A4. Moving sessions
Your plan has to fit your life. Move sessions to suit your week; the app keeps track of the load.
- Keep an easy or cross-training day after the long run when you can.
- If you move an interval session next to another one, the app will suggest turning one into an aerobic run.
- Want to replace a rest day? An easy walk or cross-training session, **30 minutes or less, RPE 1–4**, is fine.
**Remember:** the app recommends, you decide.

### A5. When a session feels too hard
Some days you won't have it. That's normal.
1. Lower the RPE first.
2. If it's still too much, walk.
3. Log how it felt. The app uses it to set the next sessions.
**Remember:** a session done at the right effort beats a session forced at the wrong one.

## B. Effort (RPE)

### B1. What RPE means
RPE (rate of perceived effort) is how hard a session feels to you, on a 1–10 scale. It takes in your breathing, heart rate, muscles and overall effort. It adjusts for heat, hills, tiredness and terrain, so the session's purpose stays right even when the day isn't perfect.
A quick check is your breathing and talking: if you can chat easily, it's easy; if you can only say a few words, it's hard.
**Remember:** stick to the RPE in each session. Each effort trains a different system.

### B2. The app's effort scale
| RPE | Feel | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Recovery: very easy, chatty | Recovery sessions, warm-ups, cool-downs |
| 5–6 | Easy: comfortable, conversational | Easy runs, most of your weekly volume |
| 6–7 (up to 8) | Steady: noticeable breathing, still controlled | Long runs (5–6 for most, 6–7 for advanced) |
| 8–8.5 | Moderately hard: sustainable but honest | Threshold work |
| 8.5–9.5 | Hard: short focused efforts | Above-threshold work |
| 9.5–10 | Very hard: near max | VO2max and fast, short work |

### B3. Training is a spectrum, not boxes
Your body works along one continuous range of effort, from a gentle jog to an all-out sprint. Effort levels are a shared map between you and your coach, not strict boxes. What matters is the purpose of each session: which muscle fibres it uses, how much fatigue it creates and what it's building.

### B4. Recovery vs easy: why the difference matters
Recovery (RPE 1–4) costs your body almost nothing: it lets your muscles and nervous system recover while you keep moving. Easy (RPE 5–6) is a real training stimulus that builds your aerobic engine (more mitochondria, more capillaries, a stronger heart) without the fatigue cost of harder work.
Running recovery days too hard blunts recovery. Running everything too easy leaves fitness on the table.

## C. Your sessions

### C1. Long run
Builds your aerobic engine and **durability**: your body's ability to handle load again and again without breaking down. In a Hyathlon race you run alongside heavy station work, so durability is what stops your pace fading late.
Effort: RPE 5–6 for most (6–7 for advanced). Walk breaks are fine. It grows gradually.

### C2. Quality sessions: threshold, tempo and fartlek
- **Threshold:** reps of a few minutes at RPE 8–8.5 with short recoveries. Teaches your body to clear and reuse lactate and to hold a strong, sustainable effort.
- **Tempo:** longer efforts or continuous runs at a "comfortably hard" effort, just below threshold.
- **Fartlek ("speed play"):** a continuous run with faster efforts and easier recoveries, varied by feel or landmarks.

### C3. VO2max intervals
Very hard reps of **1–3 minutes** at RPE 9–10 with **standing** recovery (stand or walk slowly, don't jog). They raise your aerobic ceiling. Jogging the recovery turns it into threshold work. Used in focused blocks, not all year.

### C4. Steady run
A continuous run just below tempo effort (RPE 6–7). Challenging but sustainable; builds base endurance.

### C5. Easy and absorption runs
Easy, relaxed running that helps you recover and absorb hard sessions. Absorption runs are the easiest of all; easy runs are slightly more effort. Both keep the effort genuinely easy.

### C6. Cross-training
Bike, elliptical, pool running, swimming, walking, yoga or pilates. Builds fitness, strength and mobility while giving your legs a break from impact.

### C7. Rest days
Rest is part of training. Your body repairs and adapts on rest days. Relax, sleep well and get ready for the next session.

## D. Hyathlon-specific training

### D1. Compromised running
A Hyathlon race isn't a fresh 8 km run. You're running on tired legs, with a high heart rate, coming straight off stations. Compromised sessions train exactly that: holding your form, controlling your breathing and settling your effort under fatigue.
We build it two ways: **alternating efforts** (changing pace while tired, like station-to-run transitions) and **sustained efforts** (locking into an effort while fatigued).

### D2. Metcon sessions
Metabolic conditioning trains your engine, your ability to push hard and your muscular endurance all at once, and how to move straight from a station into a run. The goal is to make that feeling familiar before race day.

### D3. Pacing
Race day is about execution. Start too hard and it catches up with you late. Through the program you'll learn what feels sustainable early, what's actually sustainable late, and where you tend to overcook it.
**Remember:** good pacing isn't hanging on, it's being in control from start to finish.

### D4. Running technique (economy)
Small inefficiencies cost a lot over 8 km of running under fatigue. Better economy means using less energy at the same effort: free speed. *(Coaching tiers: video technique review with your coach.)*

### D5. The taper
The taper isn't doing nothing; it's arriving ready. Volume drops so fatigue clears, while intensity stays so you feel sharp, not flat. For a Hyathlon race that's about 8–14 days.

## E. Your body

### E1. Soreness after new training (DOMS)
New or harder training often brings delayed muscle soreness. It usually peaks around 48 hours and fades within 48–72 hours. It's a normal part of adapting and eases as your body gets used to the work.
If you're not sure whether it's soreness or an injury, stop and check with a medical professional such as a physiotherapist.

### E2. Warm-up
Before every hard session:
1. **5–10 min** easy walking or jogging.
2. **Activate:** walking quad stretch (20–30 m), leg swings (about 10 each leg, each direction), hamstring sweeps.
3. **Mobilise:** skipping, air squats, high knees, butt kicks.
4. **Switch on:** 5–10 min jog with 4–6 short efforts building from RPE 2 to 4 to 6, leading into the main session.
Easy and long runs can just start easy.

### E3. Cool-down
Ease down to a jog or walk so your heart rate comes down gradually. If anything's tight, some light static stretching.

### E4. Injury risk and prevention
Most running injuries come from doing too much too soon. Research shows new runners and people with a previous injury are most at risk, and knees and lower legs are the most common sites.
- **Shoes:** get fitted at a specialist running store.
- **Build gradually:** your plan increases load at a rate your body can adapt to.
- **Stick to the plan,** even on days it feels too easy.
- **Listen to your body:** pain, discomfort or soreness that doesn't settle means stop and check with a medical professional.
- **Sleep and rest days** are where you recover and adapt.
**Remember:** better to be conservative early than on the sidelines a month later.

## F. Fuelling and hydration *(general information, not individual advice)*

### F1. Everyday fuelling
Build meals around whole foods: fruit, vegetables, lean protein, whole grains and healthy fats. Cut back on highly processed foods.

### F2. Carbohydrates
Carbs fuel training and refill your muscle glycogen. Don't fear them: eat them around training and through the day. Too little can leave you low on energy. Hard training can blunt appetite, so make sure you still eat enough.

### F3. Protein
Protein repairs muscle, tendon, bone and ligament, supports your immune system and keeps you fuller for longer. Spread it across every meal, from whole foods first.

### F4. Supplements
Food first. If you eat a varied, balanced diet, supplements add little. If you're new to training, focus on your everyday food before supplements.

### F5. Hydration
Hydration is trial and error: needs change with heat, effort, sweat rate and the day. A good starting point is **drink to thirst** and don't over-drink. Practise drinking in long sessions (taps or bottles along your route).

### F6. Race-day fuelling
- Eat carb-rich meals in the days before.
- Breakfast at least 2 hours before the start.
- For events over about 90 minutes, practise your race fuel (gels, drinks) in long sessions first to avoid stomach issues.
- After the race: carbs and protein, and rehydrate.

## G. Motivation
Motivation goes up and down. On flat days:
1. Go back to your goal and your why.
2. Break it into small goals and celebrate the wins.
3. Follow the plan: it gives direction and shows progress.
4. Find an accountability partner or group.
5. Track your progress and look back at how far you've come.
6. Reward yourself for consistency.
7. Picture yourself finishing strong.
8. Look after yourself: sleep, recovery and food.

# Beginner Running & Return to Run — Design Guide

**Purpose:** How the app programs running for beginners (from no running up to a first half marathon) and for athletes returning after a longer injury. Taken from Frate Train's own programs: *12-week 5 km Walk-Run*, *5 km Run*, *10 km Run* and *21.1 km Run*, plus real return-to-run sessions.

**Where these programs differ from the app rules, the app rules win.** In particular, the products use a 2–6 RPE scale. The app always uses its own scale (see §6).

---

## 1. Levels and where a beginner starts

The products use a level ladder: **Beginner 1 → Beginner 2 → Intermediate → Advanced 1 → Advanced 2**.

| Level | Who | Running looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner 1** | No running yet; can't run continuously | Walks, hilly walks and **walk–run** sessions. Continuous running comes later in the 12 weeks |
| **Beginner 2** | Can run in short blocks, but not 20 min continuously | Aerobic runs with walk breaks, short absorption runs, a building long run; fartlek/tempo from about week 7 |
| **Ready for longer goals** (e.g. first half marathon) | Can run **20 min continuously** (pace doesn't matter) | Standard beginner structure with a long run building to event distance |

**Starting level from onboarding:** longest run in the last 3 weeks (Q5) and runs per week (Q4):
- 0 min, or "I haven't run" → Beginner 1
- under 20 min → Beginner 2
- 20 min or more → normal programming at the athlete's training level
- If an athlete wants a longer goal but can't yet run 20 min, the plan starts with a **bridge**: about 4 weeks building to 20 min continuous.

Complete beginners also see a **pre-exercise screening** prompt at sign-up (e.g. the ESSA pre-exercise screening tool): anyone answering yes to a screening question should check with their doctor before starting.

## 2. The beginner week

- **3 run sessions** a week: one quality or main session, one absorption (easy) run, and the long run. Plus **1 cross-training** session (walk, cycle, swim, yoga, Pilates) and rest days.
- **Never 3 training days in a row.**
- The absorption run or cross-training goes **the day after the long run**.
- Rest days can be swapped for an easy walk of **30 min or less**.
- Strength follows the athlete's own choices (`06`); the products treat it as optional for beginners.

## 3. Beginner sessions (app effort scale)

| Session | What it looks like | App effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk** (steady) | 15–25 min brisk walk, not overstriding | RPE 5–6 · Easy |
| **Hilly walk** | 15–25 min, steady up the hills, recover on the way down | RPE 5–6 → 7 on hills |
| **Walk–run** | e.g. 2 × (60 s run / 60 s walk) + 4 × (30 s run / 30 s walk), building to 12 × (60 s run / 30 s walk) | Runs RPE 5–6, walks recovery |
| **Aerobic run** | e.g. 2 × 5 min steady run / 2 min walk, building to 2 × 7 or 4 × 5 min | RPE 6–8 · Steady |
| **Absorption run** | 10–16 min easy (walk-in warm-up and cool-down) | RPE 1–4 · Recovery |
| **Long run** | 12 → 28 min (5 km), up to 60 min (10 km). For Beginner 1: 2 × (5 min run + 5 min walk), building to 2 × (15 min run + 2 min walk) | RPE 6–8 · Steady; walk breaks allowed |
| **Fartlek** | 10–14 min of hard efforts with easy running between, athlete's choice of timing | Efforts RPE 8.5–9.5; easy RPE 5–6 |
| **Tempo** | e.g. 2 × 8 min hard / 2 min standing, 3 × 7 min, or 4 × 5 min | RPE 8–8.5 (Mod. Hard) to 8.5–9 |
| **Cross-training** | 15–25 min walk or other activity | RPE 5–6 · Easy |

**Warm-up (every session):** 5 min easy walk or jog → leg swings → dynamic moves (skips, air squats, high knees, butt kicks) → 5–10 min building effort into the session. **Cool-down:** easy walk or jog, optional light stretching.

## 4. Beginner progression

- **12-week shape:** general preparation (weeks 1–6) → specific preparation (weeks 7–11) → event (week 12).
- **Volume moves in waves:** two weeks up, one lighter (e.g. very low, low, medium, low, medium, high, low…). The event week is very low.
- **Intensity builds separately:** aerobic running first. Fartlek and tempo alternate from about week 7.
- **Walk–run progresses** by lengthening the run segments and shortening the walks, then adding reps.
- **Long run** grows by about 2–5 min a week, dropping in the lighter weeks.
- **If a session is too hard:** lower the effort first, then add walking. Repeating a week is fine and normal.
- One running **technique cue** per block (e.g. "Run relaxed: shoulders down, release the tension") fits the "Why this session" line.
- Plans can be written in **minutes or km**; the app uses minutes (no GPS watch needed).

**Introducing intervals (prime first):** 30 s efforts inside an aerobic run come before the first interval session, to prime the neuromuscular system.
- **Has done intervals before:** week 1 = aerobic runs with 30 s efforts (e.g. 5 × 30 s fast / 30 s easy or walk). Week 2 = add **one** quality interval session.
- **Never done intervals:** week 1 = aerobic runs only. Week 2 = aerobic runs with 30 s efforts. Week 3 = add **one** quality interval session. *(Week 3 is Claude's reading of "prime first"; coach to confirm.)*
- **Beginner 1 (walk–run):** builds to continuous running first; this sequence starts once they can run continuously.
- From then on, the usual quality-session rules apply (RPE 8+ key session).

## 5. Return to run after a longer injury (member-only, after consent)

For athletes coming back from a longer injury such as a bone stress injury. Progression follows the athlete's **medical professional**; the app gives considerations only.

**Getting back into running:** no more than 30 min. Some awareness is okay, but pain isn't. A run–walk option (e.g. 1 min run / 2 min walk) is fine if the athlete feels tentative.

**Walk–run stages** (each with a 5 min walk warm-up and cool-down, run days separated by off-feet days):

| Stage | Main set | Running time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 × (1 min run / 4 min walk) | 5 min |
| 2 | 5 × (2 min run / 3 min walk) | 10 min |
| 3 | 5 × (3 min run / 2 min walk) | 15 min |
| 4 | 5 × (4 min run / 1 min walk) | 20 min |
| 5 | 5 min easy jog warm-up + 5 × (4 min run / 1 min walk) | 25 min |
| 6 | 5 min jog + 5 × (4 min run / 1 min walk) + 5 min jog | 30 min |
| 7 | 5 min jog + 20 min easy + 5 min jog | 30 min continuous |

Then a short **easy test run** (about 30 min) before normal running resumes.

- **Moving up a stage:** only when there's no pain during the session or the next day (awareness is okay). If symptoms return, go back a stage. If they persist: "check with your medical professional".
- **Meanwhile, off-feet sessions** keep fitness: bike tempo, high-cadence bike speed, long ride, erg sessions (same zones and RPE as usual).

**After the stages, build back like a beginner:**
- Easy runs with walk breaks: 6 × (3 min jog / 2 min walk) → 6 × (4 / 1) → 6 × 5 min with 1 min walk → 20 min easy + 1–3 min walk + 20 min easy.
- Short neuromuscular efforts inside an easy run: 10 × 15 s fast / 45 s walk → 5 × 30 s fast / 30 s walk.
- Then fartlek (e.g. 60 s fast / 60 s walk, 30 s fast / 15 s walk), letting the athlete stop when needed.

## 6. Translating the products' effort scale

The products use a 2–6 RPE scale. **The app never shows these numbers**; it uses its own scale.

| Product RPE | Product feel | App effort |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Easy (normal talking) | RPE 1–4 · Recovery (absorption) or 5–6 · Easy (walks) |
| 3 | Moderate (easy talking) | RPE 5–6 · Easy |
| 4 | Steady (less easy to talk) | RPE 6–8 · Steady |
| 5 | Somewhat hard (difficult to talk, ~1 hour) | RPE 8–8.5 · Mod. Hard |
| 6 | Hard (~30 min effort) | RPE 8.5–9.5 · Hard |

## 7. Notes for the app build
- Walk–run, the bridge to 20 min and the return-to-run stages are **fixed progressions**. They can be stored as templates and stepped through (repeat or move up based on the check-in and logs) rather than generated from scratch each time. That's cheaper and more reliable.
- The weekly check-in's "repeat this week" is a normal outcome for beginners, not a failure.

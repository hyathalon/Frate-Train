# Training Methods — Zone + RPE Version

**Purpose:** Replaces Section 3 ("Detailed Training Methods") of *The Hyathlon System — Combined System + Training Methods*. Every method is now written in the same Zone + RPE language as `00-training-zones.md`.

**If this file and `00` ever disagree, `00` wins.** Athletes see RPE + feel word. Zones are internal. No generic fixed paces, splits, watts, loads, %VO2max or %HR in sessions.

RPE chart (from `00`): Z1 1–4 Recovery · Z2 5–6 Easy · Z2 6–8 Steady · Z3 8–8.5 Mod. Hard · Z4 8.5–9.5 Hard · Z5 9.5–10 Very Hard.

Race-pace references (e.g. "15 km pace") are only there to help the athlete picture the effort. They are never a pace target.

All sessions include a warm-up and cool-down at RPE 1–4 (strides or drills can be added to the warm-up).

## 2. Method notes

### Recovery / absorption run: Z1, RPE 1–4
- The day after a medium–hard session or race. It should feel almost too easy.
- The athlete can shorten it or replace it with an off-feet Z1 session (see `00` §5).

### General aerobic (easy) run: Z2, RPE 5–6 · Easy
- Easy, conversational, a bit quicker than recovery runs. This is most of the weekly volume.
- Optional strides or 30–60 s surges.

### Long run: Z2, RPE 6–8 · Steady
- Usually 50–90 min. Start at or just below the athlete's **longest run in the last 3 weeks (minutes)**, then add about 5–10 min per step with a lighter week in each block.
- Optional race-pace segments at Z3 (RPE 8–8.5) in the specific phase.
- Give a planned value with a min–max range. The next block is built from what the athlete actually completed.
- No long run in the final taper.

### Tempo / aerobic threshold: upper Z2 – low Z3, RPE 7–8
- "Marathon to half-marathon effort": controlled and rhythmic, not a race.
- Often done inside the long run (e.g. the last 20 min at RPE 7–8).
- This is where the old "Tempo" sits. It no longer overlaps with Lactate Threshold, because the two now have separate RPE bands.

### Lactate threshold / cruise intervals: Z3, RPE 8–8.5
- "15 km to half-marathon effort": the effort hybrid-race running usually sits at.
- Two forms: **cruise intervals** (2–5 min reps with short rest) or **continuous blocks** (e.g. 20 min + short easy + 10 min).
- Correction: the old document said "10 km – 21.1 km pace". 10 km effort now belongs to Critical Velocity (Z4).

### Critical velocity: Z4, RPE 8.5–9.5
- "10 km to 5 km effort": threshold's faster, punchier cousin, not all-out.
- Use once LT work is established and a fresh stimulus is needed.

### VO2max intervals: upper Z4 – Z5, RPE 9–10
- "3–5 km effort". Reps of 2–5 min (VO2 takes about 90 s–2 min to reach its ceiling).
- **Recovery is standing, about half the work time** (the old document said 1:1). A jog recovery turns it into threshold work, not true VO2max training.
- Pillar: **Aerobic Engine** (the booklet listed it under Threshold).
- Focused blocks only, once LT and CV work are established. Not a year-round staple. Monitor recovery.

### Speed endurance and sprint intervals: Z5, RPE 9.5–10
- **Sprint intervals (30 s or less) are for running economy**, not fitness: neural drive, mechanics, fast-twitch recruitment. Little and often.
- **Speed endurance (longer than 30 s) is a fitness session.** Use sparingly, mainly in the specific phase, in focused blocks.
- Full passive recovery for both, so quality stays high.
- Stop the session when mechanics break down.
- These replace the old "SIT" entries (which used %VO2max and 200 m–1500 m race paces).

### Hill sprints: Z5, RPE 9.5–10
- 8–10 s, full walk-down recovery. Neural and mechanical work, not fitness. Little and often.
- Place by time inside a run, e.g. "4×10 s hill sprints @ RPE 9.5–10 at the 20 min mark, walk-down recovery".

### Hill reps: Z3–Z4, RPE 8–9
- 1–3 min uphill at a strong, controlled effort. Builds strength endurance and fatigue resistance.
- Jog-down recovery.

## 3. Cross-training

Every method above carries over to ergs, bike, elliptical and pool running at the same zone and RPE, using the modality effort guide in `00` §5 and the manipulators in `03`. Off-feet versions of recovery, aerobic, threshold and VO2max sessions are the default when a niggle is reported.

## 4. What changed from the old Section 3

| Old | New |
|---|---|
| Intensity as %VO2max, %MHR, %HRR | Zone (internal) + RPE and feel word (athlete-facing) |
| Tempo 79–88% MHR and LT 82–91% MHR overlapped | Tempo RPE 7–8, LT RPE 8–8.5, CV RPE 8.5–9.5: separate bands |
| LT = 10 km–21.1 km pace | LT = 15 km–half effort; 10 km effort = CV (Z4) |
| VO2max 2–4 min, recovery 1:1, "88–84% HRR" (typo), Threshold pillar | 2–5 min, standing recovery about half the work time, Aerobic Engine pillar |
| Long run 74–88% MHR, 26–35 km | Long run RPE 6–8 (Steady), usually 50–90 min, set from the athlete's recent longest run |
| Recovery run <76% MHR, "60–90 sec slower than marathon pace" | Z1, RPE 1–4 |
| General aerobic "up to 16 km" | Z2, RPE 5–6 (Easy), shorter than the long run |
| Hill intervals as %VO2max | Hill sprints (Z5) and hill reps (Z3–Z4) as separate methods |
| SIT short/long at 200 m–1500 m pace | Sprint intervals (≤30 s, economy) / speed endurance (30–90 s, fitness) at RPE 9.5–10 |

**Removed as defaults (marathon-runner volumes, not right for hybrid athletes):** weekly totals of 80–113 km, 113–145 km and 145+ km; midweek long run of 18–24 km; the double-run rule (30 min minimum when the day is over 13 km); long-run paces 10–25% below marathon pace. These can be set for an individual high-volume runner by the coach.

## 5. Decisions

Settled:
1. **Zones:** Z1 Recovery RPE 1–4 · Z2 Easy 5–6 (easy runs) · Z2 Steady 6–8 (long runs) · Z3 8–8.5 · Z4 8.5–9.5 · Z5 9.5–10.
2. **Sprint intervals of 30 s or less are running economy** (Economy pillar). Longer than 30 s is speed endurance, a fitness session (Aerobic Engine). Both are now session types in `00` §4.
3. **Hill reps:** the old "increase 5 to 8 beats per min" wording is removed.
4. **VO2max is Aerobic Engine**, with standing recovery. A jog recovery makes it threshold work. (The Hyathlon System booklet still lists VO2max intervals under Threshold; update it at the next revision.)

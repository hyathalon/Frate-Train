# Compromised Running (Fatigue Resistance & Clearance) + Running Database Notes

**Purpose:** Frates's own compromised-running sessions, written in the app's language, plus the parts of his running programming workbook that are useful for the app's running database.

App rules still apply: athletes see RPE, not paces; "Hyathlon", not event brands; loads by feel; exercises from the database.

---

## Part A. Compromised running: re-composition

### A1. The idea
Compromised running here isn't about making sessions harder. It trains the ability to **re-establish good running while still metabolically and mechanically loaded**.

**DISRUPT → CLEAR → RE-ESTABLISH → RELOAD.** The athlete creates fatigue, clears enough of it while still moving, finds their running rhythm again, then gets another stressor. The goal isn't maximum fatigue. It's holding output and mechanics while the body is trying to slow down.

**Signature concept: re-composition.** The ability to reorganise breathing, posture, stride and effort after a high-stress stimulus and return to effective running, while still working. *"Can you reconstruct good running while fatigued?"*

### A2. Effort translation (athletes see these)

| Written in the source | App effort |
|---|---|
| Race pace / "Hyathlon race pace" | **RPE 8–8.5 · Mod. Hard** (athlete's own race effort) |
| 105% of race pace / "slightly faster than race pace" | RPE 8.5–9 · Hard |
| 90% of race pace / controlled float | RPE 6–7 · Steady |
| ~5 km effort | RPE 9–9.5 · Hard |
| Hard effort / surge (20–30 s, 200 m) | RPE 9–9.5 |
| Very easy / easy jog / recovery | RPE 5–6 · Easy |
| "Heavy" carry, sled, high-force exercise | By feel: a load that's hard for the time given, with good form |

If the athlete has a stored race average run pace, the app can show their own pace next to the RPE (never instead of it).

### A3. The sessions
All: warm-up RPE 1–4 building to strides, cool-down RPE 1–4. Pillar: **Durability** and **Fatigue Management**. These are **quality sessions**, usually in the specific phase.

| # | Session | Main set | Purpose / cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clear & Carry** | 4–6 rounds: 800 m race effort · 60 s recovery · 20–30 s surge · 60 s race effort · 40–60 m heavy farmer carry · straight into 400 m race effort · 2 min easy | Metabolic load → muscular fatigue → re-establish rhythm. *"Clear while still moving."* |
| 2 | **Redline → Reset → Race** | 3–5 sets: 400 m ~5 km effort · 200 m very easy · 800 m race effort · 60 s easy jog · 400 m race effort · 2 min recovery | Spike, then regain economy at race effort. *"Don't chase the pace. Find the rhythm."* |
| 3 | **Broken Race Run** | 6–8 × (300 m slightly faster than race · 200 m float · 300 m race effort · 200 m float) | Oscillate between build-up and clearance without full recovery. Change gears without losing efficiency. |
| 4 | **No Free Recovery** | 4 rounds: 1 km race effort · 500 m very easy · 1 min sled push/pull · 500 m race effort · 500 m very easy · 1 min SkiErg or row | Active recovery: clear fatigue while still moving. *"Recovery doesn't mean stopping."* |
| 5 | **Fatigue Sandwich** | 6 rounds: 500 m race effort · 30–40 s high-force exercise · 500 m race effort. Rotate: heavy sled push, farmer carry, wall balls, lunges, SkiErg, burpee broad jumps | First run sets rhythm, station disrupts, second run tests whether rhythm can be rebuilt. |
| 6 | **Durability Ladder** | 5 sets: 600 m race effort · stressor 30 → 45 → 60 → 75 → 90 s · 600 m race effort | How much stress before running deteriorates. **Don't reward faster running; test pace stability.** |
| 7 | **Load → Clear → Hold** | 6 rounds: 500 m slightly faster than race · 500 m race effort · 30 s heavy movement · 500 m race effort | The last 500 m is the test: settle straight back into economical running. |
| 8 | **Surge → Settle** | 6 × 1 km: 200 m hard · 600 m race effort · 200 m hard · 90 s easy jog | Spike demand, settle without big pace loss (the gear changes around stations). |
| 9 | **Fatigue → Clearance → Precision** | 4–5 rounds: 800 m race effort · 40 s wall balls · 400 m race effort · 60 s easy jog · 200 m slightly faster than race | Run fast with precision while loaded. Controlled, not sprinting. |
| 10 | **The Echo** | 800 m race effort → 40 s station → 800 m race effort, rotating sled, burpees, farmer carry, wall balls | First 800 m sets the pace; the second tests whether it comes back. |

**For "No running" athletes** who chose simulations (onboarding Q2c): run segments become their preferred erg or bike at the same effort.

### A4. Progression and measuring it
- **Progress by changing one thing:** stressor duration, density, movement choice, running volume, or recovery. Always protect the quality of the running.
- Not every session should be a maximal sufferfest. It should be controlled enough to show quality running under stress, repeatedly.
- **Re-composition measure (app feature):** from watch data, compare fresh vs compromised run pace and track **pace drop across repeated run segments** (plus cadence if available). Example: 4:20 → 4:22 → 4:24 → 4:28 /km shows far more durability than 4:20 → 4:35 → 4:48 → 5:00. Show it as a trend over time, e.g. "Pace held within 3% across all rounds".
- Heavy sled, lunge and carry work counts for the **interference rule** (not the day before a key run or long run).

### A5. Long runs with efforts (Frates's long-run progression)
**Build up to it:** once an athlete can run about 80 min. Before that, build the long run in bands: **50–60 → 60–70 → 70–80 min**.

Effort translation: "easy, conversational" long run = **RPE 6–7 (low end of Steady)**. "Race goal pace to +10 s" = **Hyathlon race effort, RPE 8–8.5**. "Goal pace to –5 s" = **slightly faster than race effort, RPE 8.5–9**. "Float (+30–40 s)" = **RPE 6–7**. "Easy jog" = **RPE 5–6**. If the athlete has a stored race average pace, the app can show their own paces alongside (e.g. "4 × 5 min @ 4:40–4:50 /km").

| Stage | Duration | Structure | Lever added |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LR 1** | 80–90 min | Easy long run | Time on feet |
| **LR 2** | 80–90 min | Hilly terrain: climb at an easy hill effort (harder than flat, but don't push) | Terrain |
| **LR 3** | 85–90 min | Hilly terrain: steady push up the hills (about RPE 7), recover on the way down | Hill effort |
| **LR 4** | 85–90 min | 30 min easy · 4 × 5 min race effort / 10 min easy · rest easy | Race-effort segments |
| **LR 5** | 90–100 min | 30 min easy · 4 × 5 min race effort / 3 min easy · rest easy | Shorter recovery |
| **LR 6** | 90–100 min | 30 min easy · 6 × 5 min race effort / 3 min easy · rest easy | More reps |
| **LR 7** | 90–100 min | 30 min easy · 3 × (2 min slightly faster than race / 2 min float / 1 min easy jog) · 5 min easy · 3 × (2 min slightly faster / 3 min float / 1 min easy jog) · 25–35 min easy | Alternations (gear changes) |
| **LR 7b** | 90–100 min | 30 min easy · 4 × (2 min slightly faster / 2 min float) · 5 min easy · 4 × (2 min / 2 min float) · 25–35 min easy | Continuous alternations |

- Each stage changes **one lever** (terrain → hill effort → race-effort segments → shorter recovery → more reps → alternations), matching the extend/qualify model.
- Purpose line for athletes: time on feet and aerobic capacity (mitochondria, capillaries, fat use, cardiac efficiency, durability); the hilly stages add muscular endurance and eccentric strength; the race-effort stages add rhythm and economy at race effort, plus fatigue clearance.
- Long runs of 90–100 min are for athletes ready for them (see long-run limits: most 60–90 min, advanced up to 90–120 min).
- Alternations sit in the Aerobic Engine pillar ("structured pace changes within a single run").

## Part B. Running database notes (from Frates's earlier marathon programming workbook)

### B1. Four-phase structure (always in order)

| Phase | Name | Emphasis | Quality sessions (Q1 / Q2 / Q3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Foundation & injury prevention | Easy and steady running for cell adaptation | Easy runs + strides |
| II | Early quality | Mechanics, running economy, speed | Reps or intervals / threshold / long or threshold-long |
| III | Transition quality | Toughest phase: long intervals | Intervals or threshold-long / threshold / race pace |
| IV | Final quality | Threshold, fewer reps, race specific | Threshold or race pace / threshold-long / race pace-long |

Example split for 21 weeks: 5 / 6 / 6 / 4. For Hyathlon programs this maps onto base → build → specific → taper.

### B2. Volume progression rule
Increase volume **every 3rd week**, by about **1 km per running session in the week** (e.g. 6 runs a week → +6 km). The weeks in between hold steady.

### B3. Session type vocabulary
Recovery · Easy · Steady · Long (LSD) · Medium-long · Negative split · Race pace · Turnover (strides, e.g. 15–20 × 100 m) · Tempo · Threshold / cruise intervals · Intervals · Reps · Hills · Double · Rehearsal (dress rehearsal at race time) · Fitness test.

Examples worth reusing:
- Cruise intervals: 5–6 × 5 min at threshold / 1 min recovery.
- Descending threshold ladder: 20 / 15 / 10 / 5 min threshold with 5 / 4 / 3 / 2 min recovery.
- Split long run: 12 km easy + 4 km threshold + 12 km easy.
- Dress rehearsal (race week): 15 min easy, ~4 km at race effort, 15 min easy, at the same time of day as the race.

### B4. Fitness test (repeatable benchmark)
Same flat course, e.g. 5 × ~2 km at rising heart rates (140 → 150 → 160 → 170 → 180 bpm), then one more at 150. Repeat every 6–8 weeks: **faster pace at the same heart rate = fitter**. Could become an optional app benchmark for athletes with a heart-rate monitor; results update their stored personal zone values (never generic).

### B5. Taper
- About **3 weeks: ~80% → ~60% → ~30%** of usual volume (plus the race).
- Keep some quality: turnover strides, a short tempo, a VO2max session (e.g. 6 × 800 m at ~5 km effort) two weeks out, and a dress rehearsal in race week.
- Rationale in the notes: glycogen needs 2–3 days of lower activity to top up; minor tissue repair needs at least 5 days; aerobic enzymes start to drop after about 72 h without aerobic work; hard training in the last 10 days adds little.

### B6. Useful log fields for the session log
Surface (road, footpath, crushed rock, grass, sand, trail, track, treadmill) · terrain (flat / hilly) · conditions (temperature, humidity, wind) · fuel and drinks taken · time of day · outcome (completed / fell short / did not start / did not finish). Surface and fuelling are the most useful for Hyathlon: fuelling practice on long runs, and indoor hard-floor running before races.

### B7. Race-day plan (later feature)
Pacing plan by segment, fuelling timing (e.g. a gel every ~30 min, every ~20 min late in a long race), carb-loading days, drink stations, and a pre-race checklist (breakfast 3–4 h before, toilet stops, kit). Fits the planned post-race analysis.

## Part C. Running session bank (from the 16-week marathon session bank)

The best model for the app's running library: **session families × zones**, with several variants per slot so repeat athletes (or several athletes on the same block) don't get identical plans. Fits the athlete's "variety" preference (onboarding Q14).

### C1. Weekly and block structure
- **Two quality sessions + a long run** each week (Q1 = threshold family, Q2 = faster family, LR = long run).
- **Phases:** Base (weeks 1–4) → Build (5–9) → Peak (10–13) → Taper (14–16).
- **Cutback weeks** about every 4th–5th week: trim reps ~25%.
- **Taper:** volume down ~30–40%, then further; keep frequency. Race week = freshness over fitness.
- Long runs start steady and continuous, then add race-effort pickups from the build phase; the biggest long run is about 3–4 weeks before the race.

### C2. Zone translation (the bank's pace zones → app effort)

| Bank zone (race-pace reference) | App zone | Athlete sees |
|---|---|---|
| VO2 (3 km pace) | upper Z4 – Z5 | RPE 9–10 |
| Critical speed (5 km pace) | Z4 (upper) | RPE 9 |
| Hard threshold (10 km pace) | Z4 | RPE 8.5–9 |
| Threshold (15 km pace) | Z3 | RPE 8–8.5 |
| Faster tempo (half marathon pace) | top of Steady / low Z3 | RPE 7.5–8 |
| Tempo (marathon pace) | upper Steady | RPE 7–8 |
| Steady | Z2 Steady | RPE 6–7 |
| Easy | Z2 Easy | RPE 5–6 |
| Absorption / recovery | Z1 | RPE 1–4 |

The bank calculates paces from a goal time. The app never uses those as generic paces. They can be shown next to RPE **only** as the athlete's own zone values (e.g. from their recent race result, onboarding Q6).

### C3. Quality session families
Each works in any zone above (threshold, hard threshold, critical speed, VO2, race/marathon effort):

| Family | Example (threshold) |
|---|---|
| Reps | 5 × 800 m, 45 s float |
| Ascending ladder | 2-3-4-5 min, 60 s float |
| Descending ladder | 5-4-3-2 min, 75 s float |
| Pyramid | 3-4-5-4-3 min, equal-time float |
| Broken continuous | 6 × 4 min, 30 s float (~24 min working) |
| Progression | 5–6 km continuous, steady easing into the target effort over the second half |
| Terrain fartlek | 7 × 2 min, 1 min easy, soft/varied terrain |
| Cutdown reps | 5 × 1.2 km, recovery shortens each rep |
| Compressed double | 3 × 800 m / 90 s float, 5 min jog, 3 × 1 km / 60 s float ("double threshold" in one session) |
| Sandwich | 2 km steady, 3 × 800 m at threshold, 2 km steady |
| Mixed-distance reps | 800 m, 1.2 km, 1.5 km, 1 mile, 75 s float |
| Hill repeats | 6–9 × 45–90 s uphill at threshold effort, jog down |

### C4. Long run families
Steady continuous · long run with steady (or race-effort) finish · progressive (easy easing into steady over the second half) · steady surge (one 10–20 min block on the way back) · broken steady (3–4 × 12–18 min steady with 3 min easy) · race-effort pickup long run · double pickup · negative split · easy long run (taper) · with strides.

**Hyathlon scaling:** long runs **60–90 min for most, 90–120 min for advanced** (the bank goes to 115 min for marathoners). "Marathon-pace" segments become **Hyathlon race-effort segments (RPE 8–8.5)** for Hyathlon athletes.

### C5. Where the bank conflicts with the app rules (app rules win)
- **VO2 recovery:** the bank uses short *float* recoveries for VO2 work. App rule: **standing recovery, about half the work time**; a jog float turns it into threshold work.
- **Some sessions need a coach check before import:**
  - "VO2 reps 5 × 200 m, 45 s float" is really short speed / economy work (30 s or less), not VO2.
  - VO2 ladders up to 5–7 min at 3 km pace are very long reps for that effort (VO2 reps are 2–5 min).
  - **Race week** includes a 4-5-6-7 min VO2 ladder. That conflicts with the race-week rule (a short sharpener at least 4–5 days out: 15 min just slower than race effort).
- **Coach-influence labels** (Norwegian, Didyk, Sang, Magness) are useful internally for variety, but aren't shown to athletes.
- Everything else follows the usual rules: one lever changed per session, quality sessions spaced, interference rule, etc.

### C6. How the app should use it
1. Store the **families** (C3, C4) as templates: structure pattern + zone + volume range.
2. The generator picks the family and zone for the week's purpose (phase, pillar, build/maintain), scales the volume to the athlete (their norm, level, minutes available), and progresses **one lever** from the last similar session.
3. **Rotate variants** so the same family doesn't repeat back-to-back, more often for athletes who chose "lots of variety".

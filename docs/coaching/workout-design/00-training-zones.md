# Training Zones — 5-Zone Model (Running & Cross-Training)

**Purpose:** The intensity language for every Hyathlon Performance / Frate Train session. All workout-design docs (`01`–`05`) prescribe intensity as **Zone + RPE** from this file. If any doc disagrees with this one, this one wins.

Source: Frate Train Coaching *Understanding Your Training Zones* booklet (Training Explained) and *Training Zones Reference Chart — 5 Zone Model Guide by Modality*. Built on Steve Magness's approach.

---

## 1. The big idea: a spectrum, not boxes

- Effort is **one continuous spectrum** from a gentle jog to an all-out sprint. Zones are a **map** of that spectrum and a **shared language** between coach and athlete, not a rulebook with rigid borders.
- What matters is the **purpose** of a session: which muscle fibres it recruits, how much fatigue it creates, and what adaptation it's building towards.
- Beware one-size-fits-all trends (e.g. social media "Zone 2" hype) that ignore the individual in front of you.
- Zones scale to the individual (% of their own VO2max / threshold), **never to a fixed pace, split, watt or load**.

## 2. The 5-zone reference chart

| Zone | Feel | RPE | VO2 (% max) | HR (% max) | Approx. lactate (mmol/L) | Running race-pace reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Z1** | Recovery | 1–4 | 50–65% | 60–72% | 0.8–1.5 | Recovery |
| **Z2** | Easy | 5–6 | 66–80% (across both Z2 bands) | 72–82% (across both) | 1.5–2.5 | Easy |
| **Z2** | Steady | 6–8 | | | | Steady – marathon (42.2 km) |
| **Z3** | Moderately hard — "the sweet spot" | 8–8.5 | 81–87% | 82–87% | 2.5–4.0 | 15 km – half marathon (21.1 km) |
| **Z4** | Hard | 8.5–9.5 | 88–93% | 88–92% | 4.0–6.0 | 10 km – 5 km |
| **Z5** | Very hard | 9.5–10 | 94–100% | 93–100% | 6.0–10.0 | 3 km and faster |

In plain terms:
- **Z1 (Recovery):** barely above resting; recovery sessions, warm-ups and cool-downs.
- **Z2 (Easy and Steady):** comfortable and conversational. Easy (RPE 5–6) is most of the weekly volume and the long run for beginner–intermediate runners; Steady (RPE 6–8) is steady aerobic work, and advanced runners' long runs sit at its low end (6–7).
- **Z3 (Moderately hard):** sustainable but honest, around the lactate threshold. Hybrid race (Hyathlon) running typically sits around here (≈ 15 km pace).
- **Z4 (Hard):** threshold and just above; shorter, focused efforts.
- **Z5 (Very hard):** near-maximal; used sparingly for short, high-quality bursts.

### Recovery (Z1) vs Easy and Steady (Z2): why the distinction matters
- **Z1** is barely above resting: fat is the dominant fuel, only the easiest slow-twitch fibres work, and the cost is close to zero. This is **true recovery**. It lets the adaptation from the last hard session lock in.
- **Z2** is still comfortably aerobic but a **real training stimulus**: mitochondrial density, capillarisation, stroke volume. Usually the largest share of weekly volume.
- Don't lump every non-hard day into one vague "easy". Recovery days done at Z2 blunt recovery, and everything at Z1 leaves aerobic development on the table.
- Within Z2, **Easy (RPE 5–6)** is the everyday aerobic run and **Steady (RPE 6–8)** is steady-state aerobic work. Long runs: RPE 5–6 for beginner–intermediate runners, RPE 6–7 for advanced. The upper end of Steady (7–8) overlaps with aerobic threshold work.

## 3. Four physiology concepts

1. **VO2max, the aerobic ceiling.** It is raised most by sustained hard efforts (Z4–5). But holding a high % of VO2max for a long time (a threshold and easy-volume effect) matters just as much.
2. **Muscle fibre types.** Slow-twitch (ST) fibres are endurance fibres: easy to recruit and fatigue-resistant. Fast-twitch fibres (FT-a, FT-b) produce more force and speed but fatigue faster. Harder or longer sessions recruit and aerobically train more FT fibres, which is why session types vary.
3. **Lactate is fuel, not just fatigue.** Training improves producing, clearing and reusing it. Easy work keeps lactate low and spares glycogen, and threshold work nudges it up to improve clearance. Above-threshold work is a stronger stimulus but slower to recover from, so don't overuse it.
4. **Recovery drives adaptation (GAS theory).** The body improves during recovery. Easy days and sleep are where the adaptation happens.

## 4. Session types (Magness classifications) → zones

| Session | Zone | Physiological job | When used | Coach's note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recovery / absorption** | Z1 | Easiest ST fibres only; prioritise recovery | Day (or next session) after a medium–hard workout or race | Should feel almost too easy; resist speeding up |
| **Easy ± strides or surges (30–60 s)** | Z2 Easy, RPE 5–6 (+ short pick-ups) | Aerobic base via ST fibres; light touch on FT; lactate clearance without real fatigue | Year-round foundation | Where the engine is quietly built |
| **Long** | Z2: RPE 5–6 (beginner–intermediate), 6–7 (advanced) | As easy fibres tire, harder-to-recruit fibres join, so more muscle is trained aerobically | Year-round except taper | Duration and consistency over pace (except planned race-pace segments) |
| **Progression** | Z1/Z2 → Z3 (–Z4) | Early ST fatigue forces different fibres into work at faster efforts | Year-round; versatile aerobic-to-threshold | Controlled early, honestly hard by the end |
| **Aerobic threshold** (marathon–half pace) | upper Z2 – low Z3 | More ST recruitment without high lactate | Base; often inside a long run | Controlled, rhythmic, not a race simulation |
| **Lactate threshold** (15 km / hybrid-race pace) | Z3 | ST + some FT-a; steady moderate lactate; improves clearance and reuse | Year-round; more in base and race lead-up | Often in blocks, e.g. 20 min + short easy + 10 min |
| **Above threshold / critical velocity** (10 km–5 km pace) | Z4 | More FT-a while still building aerobic capacity | Once LT work has been maximised and a fresh stimulus is needed | Threshold's faster, punchier cousin, not all-out |
| **VO2max intervals** (3 km–5 km pace) | upper Z4 – Z5 | ST + lots of FT-a and some FT-b. VO2 takes ~90 s–2 min to reach its ceiling, so reps last 2–5 min with **standing** rest ≈ half the work time | Focused blocks once threshold and CV work are established | Aerobic Engine pillar. Standing (or stationary) recovery only: a jog recovery turns it into threshold work, not true VO2max training. Quality over quantity; not a year-round staple |
| **Speed endurance** (30–90 s reps) | Z5 | Fitness: FT-a/FT-b under high lactate; holding speed and form as fatigue builds | Mainly specific phase, focused blocks | Full passive recovery (2–3× the work time); stop when mechanics break |
| **Sprint intervals / hill sprints** (30 s or less) | Z5 (short) | **Running economy**, not fitness: neural drive, mechanics, fast-twitch recruitment | Year-round, little and often | Full recovery; stop when mechanics break |

Sprints of 30 s or less are an **Economy** session. Once reps are longer than 30 s they become a fitness session (speed endurance).

None of these work in isolation. Easy and recovery work builds the base and allows adaptation, threshold raises what can be sustained, long and progression sessions build durability and fibre recruitment, and faster work adds sharpness.

## 5. Cross-training: effort guide by modality

Percentages are relative to **each athlete's own threshold** pace or power for that modality. They are not fixed numbers and not tied to a time-in-zone prescription.

| Modality | Z1 (Recovery) | Z2 (Easy–Steady) | Z3 (Mod. hard) | Z4 (Hard) | Z5 (Very hard) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Air bike** | Very light spin, minimal resistance, low RPM | Steady, controlled cadence, fully conversational | Purposeful push, breathing noticeably harder, cadence up | Threshold effort: hard but sustainable 20–40 min, high sustained cadence/watts | All-out sprint, max cadence and watts; unsustainable beyond ~1–2 min |
| **BikeErg** | Very easy spin, low resistance | Steady aerobic, ~65–75% of threshold watts | Tempo push, ~75–85% of threshold watts | Threshold power, ~85–95% of threshold watts | Max power, well above threshold; unsustainable |
| **Elliptical** | Very light resistance, easy rhythm | Comfortable steady stride, fully conversational | More resistance/incline, breathing harder but controlled | Hard sustained push near max sustainable | All-out sprint intervals; unsustainable |
| **SkiErg** | Very light pull, relaxed technique | Steady aerobic, ~65–75% of threshold split | Tempo, ~75–85% of threshold split, purposeful pull | Threshold, ~85–95% of threshold split, hard sustained | Max-effort sprint; unsustainable |
| **Rowing erg** | Very light, relaxed stroke, long recovery ratio | Steady aerobic, ~65–75% of threshold split (¾ slide if managing load) | Tempo, ~75–85% of threshold split, purposeful | Threshold, ~85–95% of threshold split, hard sustained (¾ slide if managing load) | Max-effort sprint; unsustainable |
| **Treadmill / track** | Walk or very easy jog, barely noticeable effort | Easy, fully conversational | Steady/moderate, comfortably hard | Threshold running, hard but sustainable 20–40 min | Sprint/max; unsustainable beyond short bursts |
| **Pool / aqua running** | Very light, relaxed, minimal water resistance | Steady, controlled cadence, fully conversational | Purposeful push against water, breathing harder | Hard sustained effort; a genuinely zero-impact option for threshold work | All-out, max cadence; unsustainable |

### Applying session types to cross-training
The Magness session types in §4 carry straight over to ergs and low-impact modalities:
- **Recovery / absorption:** Z1 (RPE 1–4) spin, row, elliptical or pool. Ideal the day after a hard run or race.
- **Aerobic volume:** Z2 erg/bike/elliptical. Easy (RPE 5–6) when it replaces an easy run, the long-run effort (RPE 5–6, or 6–7 for advanced) when it replaces a long run. Adds aerobic volume without running impact.
- **Progression:** e.g. SkiErg or bike building Z1 → Z3 across the session.
- **Threshold blocks:** Z3 erg blocks (e.g. 20 min + short easy + 10 min). Pool running or bike for a zero- or low-impact threshold session.
- **Critical velocity / VO2max:** Z4 reps, and Z4–5 VO2max reps of 2–5 min with stationary rest ≈ half the work time (stop or sit still, not easy spinning). Air bike or BikeErg suit these well.
- **Stations** (sled, wall balls, lunges, carries, burpee broad jumps): prescribe by Zone/RPE from the same scale. Station output is mostly judged by effort, not % threshold.

## 6. How zones are shown and used (coach decisions)

- **Athlete-facing label = RPE.** People interpret "Zone 1 / Zone 2" differently, so sessions shown to athletes use **RPE + feel word** (e.g. "RPE 5–6 · Easy"). Zones are kept internally for planning and analysis.
- **Z1 (RPE 1–4 · Recovery) is used for recovery sessions, warm-ups and cool-downs** (e.g. the recovery run the day after a hard session). It is not used for general easy running.
- **Warm-ups and cool-downs are RPE 1–4** (strides or drills can be added to warm-ups).
- **Easy runs are RPE 5–6 · Easy. Long runs are RPE 5–6 for beginner–intermediate runners and RPE 6–7 for advanced**, with optional harder segments where planned.
- Use the zone boundaries exactly as in the chart: Z1 Recovery 1–4 · Z2 Easy 5–6 · Z2 Steady 6–8 · Z3 Mod. Hard 8–8.5 · Z4 Hard 8.5–9.5 · Z5 Very Hard 9.5–10.

## 7. Athlete-specific zone values (stored per athlete, never generic)

Each athlete has a **dated** table of their current pace / power / HR per modality and zone (air bike, BikeErg, elliptical, SkiErg, rower, treadmill, track, pool). It is filled from testing or logged sessions and updated as they get fitter.

- This belongs in the **athlete's profile / data**, not in generic session templates.
- A generic session is written in **Zone + RPE**. For a specific athlete, the app or coach may show their own pace/power/HR alongside it.
- Any example paces in the source chart (e.g. track paces) belong to one athlete and must not be reused as defaults.

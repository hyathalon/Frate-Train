# Coach Insights — Threshold, Taper, Return After Racing, Strength Types, Compliance

**Purpose:** Ideas from coaches Frates respects (The Bayens Method, The Endurox System, WDM Coaching), checked against the app rules. Where they conflict, the app rules and Frates's decisions win (e.g. the app's RPE scale; Frates keeps a 60–90 min long run). Frates's own comments are marked "Coach".

---

## 1. Philosophy that fits (adopted)
- **Hyathlon is a fatigue-avoidance event, not a fatigue-tolerance test.** The question isn't "how much fatigue can you survive?" but "how little fatigue can you create while still moving fast?" Build running efficiency, station efficiency and strength endurance so every km and every rep costs less. Pacing is fatigue avoidance: starting too hard borrows energy from the end of the race.
- **Every session has to justify its recovery cost.** Quality is concentrated, recovery is protected.
- **Threshold is a state, not a fixed pace:** the upper boundary where the body can still stabilise. Training moves that boundary faster. Three levers: aerobic ceiling, how much of it you can use sustainably, and running economy. Easy volume builds the foundation; harder intervals raise the ceiling; strength and faster running improve economy; threshold work teaches the athlete to use all of it at a high, sustainable output.
- **Progress isn't "faster every week".** The same output getting cheaper (lower RPE or HR, less drift, more control, more repeatable) is progress. Change the session when it stops creating the stimulus (see §4).
- **Fundamentals (WDM):** find the dose you can sustain (frequency → volume → intensity); target the limiters (from race analysis, later feature), maintain the strengths; overload should be boringly progressive (stimulus → recover → adapt → slightly more); life sets the recovery budget; specific doesn't mean complicated.
- **Full race simulations are very fatiguing:** not every week. Compromised running in **small, frequent doses** inside quality days gives specificity without the recovery cost. **Coach decision:** the recommendation is a full simulation every 3–4 weeks, but **the athlete decides** (onboarding Q2d): some do them weekly for the last 6 weeks, every 2nd week, or once a month at the end of a block. If they have their own plan, the app follows it; if not, it follows the 3–4 week rule.
- **Ergs (SkiErg, rower) are modalities, not punishments:** build frequency first (easy, often, technique), then volume, then intensity. The SkiErg costs more locally than heart rate suggests. The goal is an output that's fast enough, cheap enough and repeatable enough to run well afterwards.

## 2. Long run (Coach decision)
- Keep a long run: **60–90 min for most** (about 16 km easy is "long" for an ~8 km race). It builds economy and durability; kept easy, it won't meaningfully hurt strength or muscle.
- **Efforts in the long run only for well-conditioned athletes, never beginners** (see `08` A5 stages LR 4+).

## 3. Reference week (Bayens) and the Hyathlon version
Bayens's 5-run week: Mon aerobic + strides · Tue tempo + compromised · Wed no-impact easy · Thu threshold + compromised · Fri no-impact easy · Sat threshold + compromised · Sun very easy restorative.
**App version (Coach keeps the long run):** use this shape for intermediate/advanced athletes, with **one of the Sat/Sun slots as the 60–90 min long run**. Compromised doses (work → run → work → run) are built into the quality days. "Tempo" in the app means sub-threshold to marathon effort (RPE 7–8).

## 4. Repeating quality sessions (Coach decision)
- A quality session may be **repeated unchanged 2–3 times** while the athlete's response improves (same output at lower RPE or HR, or less drift). Then change one lever.
- **The athlete chooses** at onboarding (and in "Plan your next block"):
  - *Same session two weeks in a row, then progress*
  - *Alternate weeks* (A / B / A / B, each progressing on its repeat)
  - *Something different each time* (change one lever every session)
- Easy, recovery, optional and maintain sessions can always repeat.

## 5. Taper (Coach decision)
- **Hyathlon races scale with training volume (Coach decision).** Low-volume athletes (3 or fewer runs a week): about 7 days; not much changes except session types (e.g. last long run with efforts about 6 days out, then an easy race week). Higher-volume athletes: **8–14 days.** Longer after a bigger training block. Volume down **40–60%**, e.g. normal threshold 8 × 4 min → taper 4 × 4 min; normal long run 75 min → 35 min.
- **Keep intensity and keep frequency:** train as often, just less each time. Short hard efforts keep legs and nervous system switched on.
- **Strength tapers for power, not just volume:** sled, wall balls and lunges need fresh, fast muscle. Maintain load with fewer sets and reps done fast (see `06`).
- Watch readiness (sleep, HRV if available, energy, fatigue) through the taper.
- **Marathon goals** keep the longer taper by program length (12+ weeks ~3 weeks: ~80% → ~60% → ~30%; 6–11 weeks 2 weeks; up to 5 weeks race week + 1). **Half marathon (Coach decision):** under 8 weeks → 1 week; 8+ weeks → 2 weeks.

## 6. Returning to hard training after a race
- A Hyathlon race is a big systemic stress: heart, muscle damage, heat, dehydration, nervous system. Feeling OK doesn't mean recovered; motivation often returns before the nervous system.
- **No intensity in the first 48–72 h** after a maximal Hyathlon race. For most athletes, quality returns **between day 4 and day 7** (the app's "1 week" rule is the default).
- **Two races in one weekend** (e.g. singles + doubles, or a relay) need more: *proposed default about 10 days before quality.* Hot conditions, a very deep effort, poor sleep or travel also mean more recovery.
- **Test the system before the first hard session back:** longer warm-up, a few controlled strides, check pace vs breathing and HR. If it doesn't feel normal, the session becomes easy aerobic instead. That's good decision-making, not weakness. The app shows this as a pre-session check on the first quality session after a race.
- **Events can hold several races** (singles, doubles, mixed, relay in one competition): the athlete adds them to one event, and recovery scales with the number of races.

## 7. Strength types (Coach decision: 2–3 × 6–10 stays the default; each type has its own dosing)
Different strength types solve different problems; the app tags exercises by type and doses each type. *Dosing below is proposed; coach to confirm.*

| Strength type | What it's for in Hyathlon | Typical dose | Example exercises |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General strength** (default) | Foundation | 2–3 working sets × 6–10 reps, "hard, with intent: 1–2 good reps left", rest 90 s–2 min | Squat, RDL, split squat, row, press, pull-up |
| **General strength / hypertrophy** | Muscle and tissue capacity; robustness; building a base | 2–3 working sets × 8–12 reps, hard with intent (1–2 good reps left), rest 60–90 s | Goblet squat, split squat, RDL, step-ups, rows, presses, pull-downs, calf raises |
| **Maximal strength** | Raises the ceiling when race loads are a big % of max (sled, carries) | 2–4 sets × 3–6 reps, heavy with good form, rest 2–3 min | Back/front squat, trap-bar deadlift, heavy sled, weighted pull-up |
| **Explosive / power** | Fast force: wall balls, burpee broad jumps, accelerating back into the run | 3–5 sets × 3–5 reps, moved fast; full rest 2–3 min; stop when speed drops | Jump squats, med-ball throws, KB swings, broad jumps, short sled sprints |
| **Reactive (plyometric)** | Cheaper running: absorb, reverse, reapply force each stride | 20–60 foot contacts a session (beginners 20–40), short ground contact, full recovery; 1–2×/week, little and often | Pogo hops, skips, bounds, hurdle hops; drop jumps for advanced |
| **Isometric** | Keeps trunk, grip and posture organised under fatigue; tendon resilience | 3–5 × 20–45 s holds (or 5 × 5 s max-intent), rest 60–90 s | Wall sit, split-squat hold, calf iso, plank / side plank, farmer hold, dead hang |
| **Strength endurance** | Your 80th rep still useful | 12–20+ reps or 30–90 s timed, or race-specific sets (20–30 wall balls, 20–50 m lunges), rest 30–90 s; a hard session | Wall balls, sandbag lunges, sled push/pull at race load, burpee broad jumps |

- **Phase emphasis (proposed):** base: general, general strength/hypertrophy (8–12), maximal + isometric; build: maximal + explosive + reactive; specific: strength endurance + power at race speed; taper: low-volume explosive/power (fresh and fast).
- Reactive and explosive work is **neural**: little and often, done fresh, stop when quality drops. It can sit early in a strength session or after a warm-up on a quality day.
- Limits for each type replace the 6–10 rep check for that type only; general strength keeps 2–3 × 6–10.

## 8. Compliance formats for easy days (Coach decision: use when motivation is low)
If two methods give a similar aerobic outcome, use the one the athlete will actually do. When the weekly **motivation** slider is low, easy sessions switch to these formats (same purpose, still easy overall), then switch back when motivation recovers:

| Format | Structure (app effort) | Best on |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous easy | 45–65 min at RPE 5–6 | Run, bike, SkiErg, row |
| Aerobic intervals | 40–70 s at RPE 6–7 / 20–40 s at RPE 1–4, repeated | Bike, rower, SkiErg |
| Short faster intervals | 15–20 s at RPE 8–8.5 / 40–70 s at RPE 1–4 | Rower, SkiErg, bike |
| Surge intervals | 5–10 s at RPE 9 / 40–60 s at RPE 1–4 | Any |
| Pick-ups in an easy run | every 5–6 min, 40–50 s at RPE 8, then back to easy | Run |

The surges are hard; the session isn't. These never turn an easy day into a hard day.
